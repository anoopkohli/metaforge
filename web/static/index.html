Many companies, one app, and AI that cannot mix them up

metaforge is a pretend business app shared by five made-up companies (tenants): a taxi company, a law firm, a dental office, a builder and a coffee roaster. Each has its own staff, records and documents. Hidden inside every company's data is a unique secret marker, a canary. If one company ever sees another company's canary, that is a leak, and the tests catch it.

On top of that app sit five AI features. Each was built the same way: list what could go wrong, write attacks that try it, then build the feature until the attacks fail.

Inside each company, people log in as staff or manager. Some fields, like salaries, patients' dates of birth and billing rates, are restricted to managers. The AI cannot get around that, whatever it is asked.

Data model stages 1–2

Describe a new record type in plain English; the AI drafts it. Every draft is checked, and nothing changes until you approve it.

Ask documents stage 3

Ask questions about your company's policies. Answers only ever use your company's documents, and cite them.

Ask data stage 6

Ask questions about your company's records. The AI writes a checked query, never raw SQL, and restricted fields stay restricted.

Incident stage 5

Break something on purpose, then watch an AI investigate, explain the cause and suggest a fix that only you can approve.

Security lab

Run every attack against the real code, and against a deliberately unsafe version, side by side.

Evals stage 4

The automatic report card that runs on every change and blocks anything that makes results worse.

Try this

  1. Sign in as a Driver at fjord and open the Assistant. Ask about your own rides: you only ever see yours.
  2. Sign in as the Receptionist at gable and ask the Assistant to mark an appointment completed. Approve it.
  3. Sign in as a manager and open the Audit log: every question, step and approval is there. Verify the chain.
  4. Sign in at fjord and open Ask documents. Ask about the expense limit.
  5. Switch to cinder and ask the same question. Different company, different answer.
  6. In Ask data, ask about average salaries as staff, then sign in as a manager.
  7. In Incident, start a queue backlog with the malicious instruction ticked, and run the Fooled AI. Watch its injected moves bounce off.
  8. In the Security lab, run a suite and compare the secure and unsafe columns.

Everything here runs on this computer with synthetic data. Without an API key, the AI is a recorded or scripted stand-in; with one, it is Claude.

What you are looking at

One assistant for everything: it can search your company's documents, query its data (several queries if needed), and propose changes. It works as you: it sees only what you can see (a driver sees only their own rides; staff never see restricted fields), and anything it proposes waits for you to approve. Every question and step is written to the audit log.

What you are looking at

Your company's record types. Platform objects are shared by every company (each can add, hide or relabel fields for itself); custom objects belong to your company only. The AI can draft changes, but its reply is treated as untrusted: it must match a strict format, may only refer to objects your company can see, and is sent back with the errors (up to 3 tries) if it fails. Nothing changes until you approve.

      The report card. Each suite runs a fixed set of cases and compares the results with a saved baseline. It fails if any single case that used to pass now fails, if the test cases were quietly edited, or if prompts grow by more than 5%. This runs automatically on every change pushed to GitHub.
